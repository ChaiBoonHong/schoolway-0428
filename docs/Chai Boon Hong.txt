ABSTRACT 
 
School transportation management in Malaysia remains heavily reliant on manual 
processes, leaving parents without real-time visibility into their children's whereabouts 
and exposing students to safety risks during transit. Existing school bus monitoring 
solutions mainly focus on GPS -based location tracking, but often lack integrated 
attendance verification, role -based communication, and school -specific operational 
workflows. As a result, coordination among parents, drivers, and school administrators 
remains fragmented, leading to issues such as missed pickups, delayed updates, and 
uncertainty over whether students have boarded or drop off safely. The objective of this 
project is to develop SchoolWay, a mobile-based school bus tracking and management 
system that provides real-time GPS tracking, automated attendance recording via NFC 
scanning, and instant push notifications to bridge the communication gap between 
parents, drivers, and administrators. Unlike existing systems that typically provide only 
location tracking, SchoolWay combines real-time GPS tracking, NFC-based attendance 
recording, and instant push notifications in a unified platform with role -based access 
for parents, drivers, and administ rators. SchoolWay is built using Flutter for cross-
platform mobile development, Firebase as the backend for real-time data 
synchronisation and authentication, and Google Maps API for live bus location 
rendering. The system's success will be evaluated through functional testing, user 
acceptance testing (UAT) with target users, and performance benchmarking of real-
time update latency. This project contributes a practical, scalable solution to a gap in 
school safety infrastructure, empowering parents with transparency, reducing 
administrative overhead, and ultimately enhancing the safety and accountability of 
school bus operations in Malaysia. 
 
Area of study: Mobile Application Development, NFC Technology Systems, Real-
Time System 
 
Keywords: School Bus Tracking System, Real-Time GPS Tracking, Flutter, Firebase, 
NFC Technology, Push Notifications, Mobile Application, Role-Based Access Control

1.1  Project Background 
School transportation is an essential support service in the education sector because it 
ensures that students travel safely and punctually between home and school. In many 
schools, transportation management is still conducted using conventional methods such 
as manual attendance recording, phone calls, and informal communication between 
parents, drivers, and school administrators [1] . Although these practices are commonly 
used, they often lead to several operational limitations, including delayed information 
sharing, inaccurate attendance records, and the absence of real-time monitoring of 
school bus movements. Therefore, parents may experience anxiety regarding their 
children’s safety, while schools and drivers may face difficulties in maintaining 
efficient and accountable transportation operations [2]. 
Recent developments in mobile computing, cloud-based systems, Global 
Positioning System (GPS) technology, and Near Field Communication (NFC) have 
created opportunities to improve school transportation management. A mobile-based 
platform integrated with GPS can provide real-time bus location tracking, enabling 
parents and school administrators to monitor journeys more effectively [3] . At the same 
time, NFC-based attendance recording can automate student check-in and check-out 
processes, thereby reduce human error and improve data accuracy [4] . Cloud services 
further support instant data synchronization and notification delivery, allowing 
important updates to be shared immediately with relevant users [2] . These technologies 
offer clear benefits to users and administrators by improving transparency, reducing

1.2  Problem Statement and Motivation 
School transportation is an essential support service within the education sector, as it 
ensures that students are conveyed safely, punctually, and consistently between home 
and school. In parallel with the advancement of digital technologies, parents and school 
authorities now place greater emphasis on service transparency, timely information, and 
operational reliability. Nevertheless, many school transportation services continue to 
depend on conventional and largely manual practices, resulting in a mismatch between 
parents’ expectations and the actual level of service provided. This situation 
underscores the need to examine the limitations of existing school bus management 
practices and to justify the development of a more systematic and technology-driven 
solution [5] . 
A major issue in current school transportation management is the absence of 
real-time visibility of school bus movements. In many cases, parents and school 
administrators rely on phone calls or text-based communication to obtain updates about 
bus location, arrival times, or unexpected delays. Such methods are inefficient, 
inconsistent, and highly dependent on the immediate availability of the driver. This lack 
of accurate and timely tracking information can create anxiety among parents, reduce

Another significant problem is the continued use of manual methods for

arrivals, reduced parental uncertainty, improved route coordination, and better 
accountability in student movement [14].

practices. Parents often depend on phone calls or informal messaging to confirm 
whether a bus has arrived, while drivers or attendants may record attendance manually 
using paper-based lists or verbal checks. These approaches are simple to implement, 
but they are also limited in accuracy, speed, and transparency [15]. Manual records are 
vulnerable to miss and human error, and conventional communication methods do not

2.1.2  Mobile Platforms and Development Framework 
Mobile applications are typically delivered through operating systems such as Android 
and iOS or built using cross-platform frameworks that allow a single codebase to target 
multiple operating systems. The choice of platform and framework significantly affects 
project cost, development speed, hardware access, user reach, maintainability, and 
long-term scalability. Since SchoolWay is intended to provide mobility, real-time 
updates, and interaction with device features such as GPS and NFC, the selection of an 
appropriate mobile platform and development framework is a critical architectural 
decision. 
Native development on Android or iOS offers deep access to device features 
and high platform-specific performance. However, building separate native 
applications for each platform often increases the development workload and 
introduces duplication in implementation and maintenance. Cross-platform 
frameworks, by contrast, aim to reduce development overhead while keeping 
acceptable performance and access to native capabilities [17]. In the context of a 
university project with a defined scope and duration, a cross-platform framework is 
often more practical if it can support the required device integrations.

2.1.3  Student Attendance and Identification Technologies 
Student attendance and identification are central to the SchoolWay mobile application 
because the project aims to automate the recording of boarding and exiting events [4]. 
In transportation settings, an effective attendance technology must be accurate, fast, 
easy to use, and practical in real-world boarding conditions. Several approaches can be 
considered, ranging from purely manual methods to contactless digital identification 
mechanisms such as QR codes, RFID, and NFC [7]. 
The selection of an identification technology should consider more than 
technical functionality alone. It should also reflect operational constraints such as 
scanning speed, physical proximity, reliability in mobile environments, accidental 
misreads, ease of deployment, and suitability for school-aged users. For this reason, a 
comparison of alternative attendance approaches is necessary before selecting the most 
appropriate method for SchoolWay. 
 
2.1.3.1  Manual Attendance Recording 
Manual attendance recording typically involves checking student names from a printed 
list, marking attendance by hand, or using verbal confirmation during boarding and 
drop-off. This method has been widely used because it is simple, requires no specialized 
equipment, and can be implemented immediately without software infrastructure [4]. 
Its primary strength lies in low cost and ease of use. Since it does not depend on 
network access, mobile devices, or scanning technology, manual attendance can be 
performed in nearly any environment. It also allows human judgment in situations 
where student identity needs to be confirmed directly. 
However, the weaknesses of manual attendance are large. This approach is  
prone to human error, illegible records, delayed updates, and inconsistent information 
[4] . It is also difficult to share in real time with parents or administrators and creates 
unnecessary administrative workload for drivers or transport staff. In the context of 
school transportation, where timely and accurate confirmation is important, manual 
attendance is inadequate for scalable and accountable operations [4], [7]. 
 
2.1.3.2  QR Code-Based Attendance 
QR code-based attendance uses printed or digital QR codes that are scanned by a mobile 
device camera to identify a student and record an event [15]. This method is widely

used in educational and event management systems because QR codes are inexpensive 
to generate and easy to distribute [15]. 
The strengths of QR code systems include low implementation cost, minimal 
hardware requirements beyond a smartphone camera, and ease of integration into 
software applications. QR codes can also encode unique identifiers and work well for 
check-in workflows where controlled scanning conditions are available [15]. 
Nonetheless, QR-based attendance has practical weaknesses in transportation 
environments. Camera-based scanning may be slower than tap-based approaches, 
especially when lighting conditions are poor, camera focus is inconsistent, or printed 
codes are damaged [15]. Students may also need to position their code correctly, which 
can create queueing during boarding. In addition, QR codes can be shared or duplicated 
more easily than short-range tap interactions, which may reduce security in some 
situations. Therefore, while QR codes are practical in many settings, they are not always 
ideal for fast and consistent bus boarding workflows. 
 
2.1.3.3  RFID-Based Attendance 
RFID-based attendance relies on radio frequency identification tags and readers to 
detect a student’s presence or identity [7]. RFID systems are widely used in access 
control, inventory management, and automated check-in scenarios. In school 
environments, RFID cards or tags can be assigned to students and detected by a reader 
mounted near an entrance [7]. 
A key strength of RFID is its speed and contactless operation. It can support 
automated identification with minimal user interaction and is effective in environments 
where repeated, routine scanning is required. Depending on the type of RFID system 
used, tags can be read quickly and from varying distances [7]. 
However, RFID also has limitations. Dedicated RFID readers are typically 
required, increasing hardware cost and integration complexity [7]. Longer reading 
ranges, while useful in some contexts, may lead to unintended reads if multiple tags are 
detected at once. This can reduce precision in transportation use cases where intentional 
confirmation of boarding or exiting is preferred [7]. RFID systems may therefore be 
effective but less practical for a mobile-app-centred system that aims to leverage 
standard smartphone capabilities rather than additional hardware infrastructure.

2.1.3.4  NFC-Based Attendance 
NFC-based attendance uses Near Field Communication to identify a student when an 
NFC-enabled tag or card is tapped against a compatible mobile device [25], [26]. NFC 
is a short-range communication technology designed for close-proximity interaction, 
which makes it suitable for intentional, secure, and quick event logging [26]. 
Its strengths are closely aligned with the needs of SchoolWay. NFC interactions 
are fast, simple, and user-friendly, requiring only a short intentional tap [25] . The short 
communication range reduces the likelihood of accidental reads and supports clearer 
event confirmation than longer-range methods [26]. NFC can also be implemented 
using widely available Android devices equipped with NFC capability, which reduces 
the need for separate dedicated readers in a prototype environment [25]. In a boarding 
process, this makes NFC efficient and operationally suitable [25]. 
The weaknesses of NFC include dependency on compatible devices and the 
need to manage tags or cards assigned to students. Some mobile platforms or device 
models may impose restrictions on NFC usage, and deployment must account for 
hardware compatibility. Even so, in a system focused on controlled attendance capture 
during transport, NFC provides a strong balance between usability, accuracy, and 
implementation feasibility [26].

The comparison of attendance technologies shows that manual attendance is the least

but often requires dedicated reader hardware and may be less precise if tags are read 
unintentionally. NFC provides a more deliberate short-range interaction, making it 
especially suitable for recording bus boarding and exiting events in a controlled, 
intentional manner.

2.1.4  Real-Time Tracking Technologies 
Real-time tracking technologies are essential to SchoolWay because one of the main 
objectives of the mobile application is to provide continuous bus location updates to 
parents and administrators [14]. An effective tracking technology must be capable of 
supporting route-scale movement visibility, timely updates, and integration with mobile 
applications. Since school transportation occurs primarily in outdoor road environments, 
tracking must also remain meaningful over relatively large geographical areas rather 
than short indoor distances. 
Several technologies can be used to monitor movement or location, including 
GPS-based tracking, GSM or SMS-assisted tracking, and proximity-based methods 
such as Wi-Fi or Bluetooth. These technologies differ in accuracy, communication 
model, infrastructure requirements, and suitability for real-time monitoring in 
transportation scenarios [8]. 
 
2.1.4.1  GPS-Based Tracking 
GPS-based tracking uses satellite positioning to determine the geographic location of a 
device. It is widely used in navigation, fleet management, transportation monitoring, 
and ride-sharing services [14]. In mobile applications, GPS can be combined with 
network connectivity to transmit device coordinates to a backend service, where the 
location can then be visualized on a map for end users [10]. 
The major strength of GPS is its suitability for outdoor location tracking across 
large distances [8]. It can provide continuous position updates as a bus move along its 
route and supports map visualization, route awareness, and estimated movement 
monitoring. Since smartphones commonly include GPS hardware, implementation is 
practical in mobile-based systems without specialized external tracking devices [4]. 
Its weaknesses include battery consumption, signal limitations in tunnels or 
dense urban environments, and dependence on internet connectivity for real-time 
transmission [27]. GPS may also require location permission handling and optimization 
to balance accuracy against battery usage. Despite these considerations, GPS remains 
the standard and most suitable approach for route-scale outdoor transport tracking [10].

2.1.4.2  GSM / SMS-Based Tracking 
GSM or SMS-based tracking generally uses cellular communication to send location-
related information or status updates from a device to another system or user [7]. In 
some implementations, GSM is combined with GPS, where GPS obtains coordinates 
and GSM or SMS is used as the communication channel to transmit them [5]. 
The strength of GSM or SMS-based tracking is its ability to operate in 
environments where internet-based data services may be limited or unreliable. SMS 
notifications can be useful for simple event-based status alerts, such as notifying a 
parent that a bus has departed or reached a checkpoint [7]. This method can also be 
easier to understand in low-bandwidth settings. 
However, GSM or SMS alone is not ideal for rich, continuous real-time tracking. 
It does not inherently provide map-based live visualization and is less efficient for 
frequent updates. Costs may accumulate if message-based communication is used 
repeatedly, and the user experience is less interactive than that of an application 
displaying live movement on a map. Therefore, while GSM or SMS is useful as a 
communication support mechanism, it is less suitable as the primary tracking model for 
SchoolWay [5]. 
 
2.1.4.3  Wi-Fi / Bluetooth-Based Proximity Tracking 
Wi-Fi and Bluetooth-based proximity tracking are commonly used to determine 
whether a device is near a known access point, beacon, or another device [28]. These 
approaches are well suited to indoor localization, room-level presence detection, or 
checkpoint-based monitoring in controlled environments. 
Their strengths include low-power operation in some configurations and 
usefulness for short-range proximity detection. Bluetooth beacons, for example, can 
help determine whether a device has entered or left a specific zone [28]. In limited 
spaces, these technologies can support localized presence awareness. 
Their weaknesses make them unsuitable as primary bus route tracking 
technologies. Proximity-based systems do not provide continuous geographic 
coordinates over long outdoor routes and usually depend on additional infrastructure 
such as beacons or known networks. They are better suited to location approximation 
near fixed points than to full route-scale transport visibility [28]. Consequently, their 
role in SchoolWay, if any, would be secondary rather than central.

2.1.5  Database and Backend Services 
Database and backend services are essential to SchoolWay because the mobile 
application must store user data, attendance logs, bus location records, and notification-
related information [4], [10]. The backend also plays a critical role in synchronization 
among multiple users, especially when parents, drivers, and administrators need to 
access updated information at different times and from different devices [5], [12]. The 
choice of database architecture therefore affects not only storage but also 
responsiveness, scalability, and ease of integration [14]. 
For a mobile-based system such as SchoolWay, the backend should support 
structured data storage, secure access, and timely synchronization [10]. The 
technologies considered in this review are SQLite, MySQL, and Firebase, each of 
which reflects a different architectural approach to application data management. 
 
2.1.5.1  SQLite 
SQLite is a lightweight relational database engine commonly embedded within mobile 
or desktop applications [29]. It is designed for local data storage and is often used when 
an application needs offline persistence without a separate database server [29], [30]. 
Its main strength is simplicity. SQLite is easy to deploy, fast for local operations, 
and suitable for storing app data directly on a user’s device [29]. It is also efficient for 
caching, offline data retention, and local transactional storage [30]. Because it does not 
require a network connection or external server, it is useful for device-level persistence 
[29]. 
However, SQLite is not designed to function as a multi-user real-time backend 
[30]. It does not provide native cloud synchronization, centralized access for multiple 
users, or built-in authentication [29]. In the context of SchoolWay, where parents, 
drivers, and administrators require shared access to updated records, SQLite alone is 
insufficient as the primary data platform [10], [29]. 
 
2.1.5.2  MySQL 
MySQL is a widely used relational database management system that supports 
structured data storage, server-side access, and multi-user applications [30]. It is 
commonly used in web and enterprise systems where a centralized database server is 
required [30].

applications requiring map display and navigation-related features [5]. 
One of its major strengths is its accuracy and maturity. Google Maps API offers 
reliable map data, familiar visual presentation, and extensive documentation for 
developers. It also supports real-time map interaction, making it suitable for 
applications that require users to monitor moving vehicles such as school buses [12]. 
In addition, the widespread familiarity of Google Maps may improve usability, as many

2.1.6.3  Push Notification Services

addition, permission handling must be managed properly so that users allow the 
application to receive notifications. Despite these considerations, push notification 
services remain an essential component for improving user awareness and

2.2  Review of Existing Systems / Applications 
This section reviews existing systems and applications relevant to school transportation 
tracking, student attendance monitoring, and integrated transport management. 
Examining real-world applications is important because it reveals current industry

school transport management platform. The purpose is not only to describe these 
systems but also to identify the functional gaps that justify the development of

2.2.1  Existing System A: School Bus Tracking Application 
A suitable example of a school bus tracking application is SafeStop, a mobile-focused 
platform designed to provide parents and schools with visibility into school bus 
movement and arrival status [35]. SafeStop is known for enabling parents to track bus 
routes and receive updates associated with daily transportation [35]. 
The main features of SafeStop include live bus location monitoring, route 
awareness, estimated arrival support, and status updates through a user-accessible 
mobile interface [35]. From a user perspective, the system is valuable because it reduces 
uncertainty around bus arrival and allows parents to make better decisions regarding 
pickup or readiness [5]. The platform reflects a strong emphasis on real-time transport 
visibility [35]. 
Its strengths lie in usability and direct problem relevance [10]. SafeStop 
addresses one of the most common pain points in school transportation, namely the lack 
of location transparency [5]. By focusing on route tracking and updates, it improves the 
parent experience and reduces the need for repeated phone-based inquiries [35]. Its 
mobile-centred approach also aligns with the practical expectation that transport 
information should be accessible on demand [10]. 
However, SafeStop also presents limitations when considered against the 
broader objectives of SchoolWay. Its functional emphasis is primarily on tracking 
rather than integrated student attendance management [35]. It does not inherently 
resolve the challenge of verifying whether a specific student has boarded or exited the 
bus through an identification mechanism such as NFC [25]. In addition, the system may

4.4  Implementation Issues and Challenges 
During implementation, several challenges were identified: 
1. Permission orchestration complexity 
Location and notification permissions must be requested and handled safely at 
startup to avoid runtime failures in operational modules. 
2. Web runtime stability considerations 
Firestore persistence behaviour on Web required defensive configuration to avoid 
assertion-related watch failures in specific update scenarios. 
3. Security rule maturity gap 
Current rule design prioritizes development agility with authenticated access. 
Production hardening requires role -scoped authorization and stricter write 
constraints. 
4. Platform capability differences 
NFC behaviour differs by platform, and unsupported environments require graceful 
fallback handling. 
5. Real-time data consistency under intermittent network 
GPS and event updates require retry-safe logic and resilient UI states when network 
quality fluctuates. 
6. Bulk data quality management 
CSV imports require validation and controlled error handling to prevent malformed 
records from contaminating operational collections.
